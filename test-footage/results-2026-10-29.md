## Default mode: video

`pipeline.DEFAULT_DETECTOR = "video"` (the variable `SM_DETECTOR=image` brings back the previous one). Why:
1. On all 13 rows video is no worse than images, and on r03 warehouse-team it is better (14 of 20 against 10 of 20).
2. Only video has track ids.

The price is higher. Video is billed for every frame (24–30 fps), images only for 1 fps. On three clips 4 prompts cost $1.23 as video against $0.58 as images (232 requests at $2.50 per 1000). Sending video at a lower fps (for example 5 fps) would cut the price 5–6 times. Whether SAM keeps ids at that step was not checked.

The video drawbacks that remain: the hood as a hard hat on warehouse-team and the busy-warehouse operator as two tracks.

## Per-object episodes (engine with ids, video mode)

If all detections of the objects that the rule binds (`in_zone.object`, `distance_lt.a/b`, `wearing.person`…) have a track_id, the episode belongs to the objects on which the rule is true: the person for r02, the forklift and person pair for r03. Each such set has its own `idle → candidate → active` machine, so the `min_duration_s` window and the `max_gap_s` gap are counted per person. `count_in_zone` and `machine_state` do not bind objects. A rule without bound objects stays one episode per rule.

| Clip | Rule | Per-object events |
|---|---|---|
| forklift-operator | r02 | s2–s9 person #0 |
| warehouse-team | r02-vest | s3–s12 person #1 (the worker in the black jacket without a vest) |
| warehouse-team | r02 | s3–s12 person #1, s10–s12 person #5, s16 person #10 |
| warehouse-team | r03 | 12 events on pairs: forklift #0 with person #1, #2, #5, #7, #11, #13; forklift #1 with person #7 |
| warehouse-team | r01-forklift | s1–s2 #2, s2–s4 #1, s18 #11, s22 #7, s22 #12 |
| busy-warehouse | r11 | s1–s3 forklift #0 |

Differences from `expected.md`: the labels there are per rule, not per person.
- r02 warehouse-team: on s14–s22 the condition is true, but each time on a different id (#10, #2, #7, #11, #12), for 1–3 consecutive seconds. SAM changes the id when a person is hidden by the truss or leaves the frame. No id collects 3 consecutive seconds, so there is no single s3–s22 event. With `episodes="rule"` (engine) the previous s3–s22 comes back.
- r03 warehouse-team: 12 events instead of 5. Two people are at the reach truck at the same time, and each has their own event.

## Id bridging (`detect/tracks.py`, 29.10 evening)

A new id of the same prompt that appears no later than `max_gap_s` = 1 s after the old one disappears gets the old id if its box overlaps the last box of the old one (IoU ≥ 0.4) or the footpoints are closer than 0.3 m by the homography. Pairs are matched greedily, one to one. Each bridge is recorded in `bridges` in `<clip>.video.json`, and the SAM id is kept in `sam_track_id`.

Bridges: warehouse-team 1 (person #10 → #5 on s14, IoU 0.26, 0.3 m on the floor), busy-warehouse 4 (pallet and box), forklift-operator 0.

| Rule, warehouse-team | Before bridging | After |
|---|---|---|
| r02 | #1 s3–s12, #5 s10–s12, #10 s16 | #1 s3–s12, #5 s10–s16 |
| r03 | 12 events on pairs | the same 12 |

The goal "one r02 event s3–s22 for the worker without a vest" cannot be reached with this data, and bridging has nothing to do with it. The worker without a vest (#1) is in the frame only on s1–s12, and his event s3–s12 matches r02-vest in `expected.md`. Seconds s13–s22 in r02 come from other people without a detected hard hat: A in the coverall (#5) and people whom SAM sees for 1–3 seconds. The `expected.md` labels for r02 count one episode per rule, so there it is one piece s3–s22.

r03 stays at 12. These are different people at the reach truck (#1, #2, #5, #7, #11, #13), not one person under different ids. Only #2 has four events (s1–s6, s10–s12, s15, s18–s20). Between them he is farther than 2 m, so the per-rule episode also breaks at these points.

The engine and the API keep per-object episodes by default. Per-rule episode: `Engine(episodes="rule")`, `run_clip(..., episodes="rule")`, `/api/run` with `{"episodes": "rule"}`. r03 then gives 4 events, not 5 as on 27.10 (below).

## Page: one episode per rule (29.10, evening)

The page sends `/api/run` with `episodes: "rule"`: one alert per rule episode, and the feed is not split by person. The event still names the objects with ids on which the rule was true in the alert second: `subject` ("person #1", "forklift #0 and person #2"), `object_ids`. This shows in the alert header, the thumbnail has only their boxes, and Spark writes "Person #1 was…". The page has no switch (it has no advanced area). Per-object episodes are available in the API without a flag.
